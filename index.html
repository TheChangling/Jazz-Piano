Instructions

Songbook

Key, scale and fingering. Tap a key-signature button, or use the drop-downs, to choose a key. The staff shows the scale and the keyboard shows the fingering numbers, going up. You can switch to the left hand or to natural minor. Minor-key fingerings are the common ones, so check them with your teacher.

Chords. Type bars separated by | and chords separated by spaces, for example Dm7 G7 | Cmaj7, or build them with the selectors. Supported: maj7, m7, 7, m7b5, dim7, 6, m6, maj9, m9, 9, 13, 7b9, 7#9, major and minor triads, and slash chords such as C/E or Dm7/G (the note after the slash is the bass). Use b and # for flats and sharps.

Songs. The drop-down holds forms and public-domain tunes, with changes written from memory, so check them against your own score. “My songs” are saved in this browser on this device only. Browsers can clear saved data, so use Back up songs now and then, and keep the text in Notes. To restore, paste it into the box and press Restore from backup. Real Book charts are copyrighted, so enter the changes from your own copy.

Chart. Each chord gets the closest-moving voicing, with the progression labelled above it, plus a Roman numeral and a suggested scale. Use the drop-down under a chord to choose another voicing, and the following chords will adjust.

Hands. Choose which hand plays the chord, the style (close, shell or spread) and what the other hand does (root, root + 5th or a walking line). Orange keys are the chord and blue keys are the other hand. In left-hand chords, shell and spread voicings keep the root on the bottom. Right-hand fingers run lowest to highest, and rootless voicings (9ths, 13ths, 7♭9) leave the root to the other hand.

Melody and arrangement. Build the melody on the staff under MELODY: drag a note value (whole, half, quarter, eighth) onto a bar, or pick one and tap the staff. Height sets the pitch and position sets the beat (snapped to half beats). Use Dotted for dotted notes. The rest symbols beside the notes work the same way: drag or tap them onto the staff. Sharps and flats: the Sharp or flat buttons are Auto, ♯, ♭ and ♮. Auto follows the key signature chosen under KEY, SCALE AND FINGERING. Pick ♯ or ♭, then drop a note, or tap a note already on the staff to change it; pick Auto to go back. With Auto selected, tapping a note or rest deletes it. A whole note fills the bar, and a note that runs past a bar line is split and tied automatically. You can also type the melody in the box, one bar per chord bar, for example F4 A4 D5 B4 | C5:4: note name, octave, then :beats (default 1), b or # for flats and sharps, r for a rest, | between bars, and a ~ on the end to tie a note to the next one. C4 is middle C. The time selector beside the box sets 4/4, 3/4 or 2/4. Under ARRANGEMENT, VOICINGS AND FINGERINGS choose an arrangement: melody right with chords left (your usual), the reverse, melody in octaves, or the melody on top of a block chord with a bass note in the left hand. Green keys are the melody, orange the chord and blue the other hand. Shell voicings suit a hand that also plays melody or bass. Melody notes are moved by octaves to sit above or below the chords, and Play includes the melody.

Playback. Set the tempo and press Play. Your device’s sound must be on, and the iPad’s mute switch can silence it.

Song scales

Lists the scale for each different chord in the song on the Songbook tab, in the order they first appear. Chords that share a scale (for example Dm7 and Dm9) are grouped. Each card shows the notes on a staff and keyboard, one octave up and back down, in the right or left hand. Play uses the tempo from the Songbook tab, and Mark done lets you tick off scales as you practise them. The finger numbers under each note are suggestions: standard fingerings for the major scales, and a best-fit fingering worked out for the others (modes, diminished and altered scales), which may differ from your teacher's, so go with theirs if they disagree. Thumb is 1.

Chord changes

Pick a key and a progression. Each voicing keeps most notes still and moves the rest by a step, so your hand barely travels. The table at the bottom lists all 12 keys, and tapping a row jumps to that key. Spelling is simplified to one set of note names per key.

Exercises

Choose a key with the key-signature buttons, then the hand, an exercise and the scale degree to build it on. The staff shows the notes (treble clef for the right hand, bass clef for the left) and the keyboard shows them too. Finger numbers appear above the staff and on the keys when the exercise starts on a white key. Black-key fingerings vary from key to key, so check them with your teacher.

Arpeggios. Triads over one or two octaves, seventh chords, broken triads (root, 3rd, 5th, 3rd), and triad inversions played up and down.

Two notes together. Scales in thirds, sixths, tenths and octaves, with both notes of each pair played at the same time. These build the control you need for parallel two-note lines. I don’t show finger numbers for them because they depend on the key and the size of your hand.
